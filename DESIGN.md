# DESIGN.md

Visual and interaction rules. The goal is a site that feels authored, not generated. Every visual choice should look deliberate and specific to this person.

---

## 1. Identity

> **A digital exhibition of exceptionally crafted work, presented with editorial typography and engineering-level precision.**

**5-second objective.** Within about 5 seconds a visitor should feel: _"This person has taste, builds polished interactions, and pays obsessive attention to detail."_ Every design decision is judged against that sentence.

**Composition of the identity**

| Source | Weight | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| **C: Exhibition** (primary) | dominant | The work is the visual hero. Large, confident compositions, room to breathe, one project per viewport, wall-label metadata. |
| **A: Editorial** | supporting | Sophisticated typography, excellent spacing, editorial composition, deliberate asymmetry, strong written case studies, restraint. |
| **B: Precision** | accent | Precise technical metadata, numbers and indices as visual language, grid logic made visible where it helps, meticulous interaction states. |

**How engineering quality is communicated:** through _execution_ (interaction quality, speed, typographic precision, flawless states at every breakpoint), **not** by looking like technical documentation. B contributes detail, never costume.

**Interaction is a primary differentiator.** The first hover, the first focus ring and the first transition are part of the 5-second impression. Treat them with the same care as the hero.

### Drift guardrails

| The site must not become… | Symptom | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| A generic minimalist agency site | Huge type + empty space + nothing specific | Real work fills the frame. Metadata is precise and personal. Details reward a second look. |
| An editorial blog | Text-first pages, prose before work | Media leads every project. Prose lives in case studies, not on the home page. |
| A hacker terminal | Mono everywhere, green/black, fake CLI | Mono only for labels and numbers. No prompts, cursors or glitch effects. |
| A design gallery template | Uniform masonry/grid of thumbnails | Varied, composed layouts per project. Numbered sequence. Written context. |
| An AI-generated portfolio | Anything in §3 | Check §3 before every visual proposal. |
| An effects showcase | Motion you notice before content | Motion answers an interaction or reveals content. Otherwise it's cut. |

## 2. Principles

1. **The work is the hero; the interface is the frame.** UI recedes so the projects can lead.
2. **Content is the interface.** Words and work carry the page. Decoration has to earn its place.
3. **One idea per viewport.** Don't stack competing focal points.
4. **Systematic, then intentional breaks.** Follow the grid 95% of the time so the 5% that breaks it reads as a choice.
5. **Interaction is the proof.** Every interactive element has designed rest, hover, focus, pressed and (where relevant) loading/complete states.
6. **Details reward attention.** Optical alignment, typographic finesse and state polish should show up on second look.
7. **Fast is a feature of the design.** If an effect costs the performance budget, the effect loses.

## 3. Anti-patterns (never ship these)

These are the tells of a template or AI-generated portfolio:

- Gradient blobs, mesh/aurora backgrounds, glassmorphism, neon glow, purple-to-blue gradients, decorative gradients of any kind
- Card-heavy layouts: bento grids, identical rounded cards, hover-lift, drop shadows
- "Hi, I'm X 👋", typing/typewriter hero effects, rotating job-title text
- Emoji as icons, tech-logo walls, skill percentage bars, "years of experience" counters
- Particle/starfield/3D-blob backgrounds, custom cursors, cursor-follow spotlights, magnetic buttons
- WebGL or 3D used for spectacle, parallax everywhere, scroll-jacking
- Testimonial carousels, marquee logo strips, "Let's build something amazing together"
- Centered-everything layouts, uniform `rounded-2xl` everywhere, gray-on-gray low-contrast text
- Stock illustrations, AI-generated imagery, generic laptop/phone mockups
- Section titles like "My Skills", "Tech Stack", "What I Do"
- Terminal cosplay (fake prompts, blinking cursors, glitch text) and spec-sheet cosplay (dimension lines and crosshairs on everything)
- Uniform masonry or thumbnail grids of projects

> **Reference-replication phase.** While the site is rebuilt stage by stage from good-fella.com, the §3 anti-patterns and the §6 motion limits are suspended where the reference genuinely needs them (marquees, parallax, scroll pinning, durations of 1–3s for major transitions). Every exception is flagged in that stage's report, and the originality pass that follows decides which survive. Scrolling is smoothed with Lenis on a mouse or trackpad (ARCHITECTURE.md §6.10); touch and reduced motion keep native scrolling Color is the reference's own palette (§4).

## 4. Tokens

All tokens live in `src/app/globals.css` under Tailwind v4 `@theme`. Components use Tailwind classes that map to tokens. **Never use raw hex, px font sizes or arbitrary values (`[...]`) in components.** If you need one, add a token or justify it in a comment. Exact values are set in §9 decisions; the roles below are fixed.

### Color: black, white and signature orange

The exact palette of the good-fella.com reference, taken from its production CSS. Off-white and off-black are the structure: `#EEEEEE` canvas with `#141314` text in light, and the reverse in dark. Grey carries secondary text, translucent black or white carries rules, and orange is the signature accent: active states, marks, strong fields, selection and the brand field. Orange is never the default text color and never a page-wide canvas.

Values are exact and never altered for contrast. When a value cannot do a job in a mode, its role changes instead.

| Raw token | Value | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| `orange` | `#FB460D` | The signature: the light accent and the brand field. 3.0:1 on `white`, so large text, fields and marks only there. Carries `black` text (5.3:1). |
| `orange-bright` | `#FD551D` | The accent on dark. 5.7:1 on `black`, so text-capable there. |
| `orange-soft` | `#FD8D68` | Marks and bars; the surface on a brand field. 2.0:1 on `white`: decorative only. |
| `orange-muted` | `#FD7142` | Muted background on a brand field. |
| `black` | `#141314` | Dark structure: the dark canvas and light-mode text. |
| `white` | `#EEEEEE` | Light structure: the light canvas and dark-mode text. |
| `paper` / `stone` | `#F7F7F7` / `#D5D5D5` | Muted background / surface in light. |
| `charcoal` / `graphite` | `#1A1A1A` / `#333333` | Muted background / surface in dark. `charcoal` is also muted text on orange (5.0:1). |
| `grey` / `grey-light` | `#696869` / `#818081` | Secondary text in light / dark (4.8:1 / 4.7:1). |

Components use the semantic tokens, not the raw ones.

| Token | Role | Light | Dark | Brand field |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bg` | the page | `white` | `black` | `orange` |
| `surface` | surfaces, plates | `stone` | `graphite` | `orange-soft` |
| `surface-2` | muted background | `paper` | `charcoal` | `orange-muted` |
| `fg` | primary text | `black` | `white` | `black` |
| `fg-muted`, `fg-subtle` | secondary text, labels, metadata | `grey` | `grey-light` | `charcoal` |
| `rule` | hairlines | black 10% | white 5% | black 20% |
| `rule-strong` | borders, section rules | black 20% | white 10% | black 30% |
| `accent` | signature: active states, fields, accent text | `orange` | `orange-bright` | `orange-bright` |
| `accent-secondary` | marks, bars. Never text | `orange-soft` | `orange-soft` | `orange-soft` |
| `accent-fg` | text on `accent` | `black` | `black` | `black` |
| `focus` | the focus ring | `black` | `white` | `black` |
| `selection`, `selection-fg` | `::selection` | `orange` / `white` | `orange-bright` / `black` | `white` / `black` |
| `ink`, `canvas` | aliases of `fg` and `bg` for the hero and the menu | | | |

- **Modes.** Light is the default. Dark follows `prefers-color-scheme`, and `data-theme="light"` or `"dark"` forces a mode on any subtree. `data-theme="brand"` is the reference's orange field (its preloader). It is applied to one surface or moment, never to the whole site.
- **Hierarchy:** primary text is `fg`, and secondary text and labels are grey. Orange marks what is active or important; most of the page stays black and white.
- **Limits that follow from the values:** in light, `orange` (3.0:1) is not small text, so small accent text belongs in dark mode or at large sizes. `orange-soft` is never text. On a brand field, emphasis comes from black, because the accent is orange on orange.
- Project imagery stays the color of the work. The interface does not tint it.
- Flat color only: no gradients except functional image-loading placeholders, and no glows, blur, texture or shadows.
- **One exception: the hero's grid.** A fine grid pressed into the hero's canvas (`hero.css`, `.hero::before`). Each line is a groove: a 1px hairline in `--color-groove` with a 1px hairline of `--color-groove-lit` beside it (below a horizontal, right of a vertical: lit from the top left), and a 1px dot a touch darker where lines cross. The cell is `--spacing-grid` (32px, 40px from md, 48px from lg), centred on the hero's centre line and hung from its top edge. A mask calms it a little behind the copy (down to 60%, rising gently, never a spotlight) and is whole at the hero's edges. **It is the hero's alone.** It ran across the whole page for a while (on `body`); that was reversed, because behind every section it read as graph paper, competing with the labels and sitting behind body text. A second mask layer fades it out over the hero's last quarter (solid to 75% of its height, clear at its bottom edge), so it dissolves into the plain page below rather than ending on a line; the canvas shares the mask, so the fade holds while it bends. `--groove-grid` and `--groove-grid-position` stay in `globals.css` for the hero to draw. Light: a faint shade with a white lit edge on off-white; dark: a deeper shade with a barely-there lit edge on off-black. No orange. It is drawn with hard-stop CSS backgrounds on one non-interactive pseudo-element (the gradient functions draw lines and the mask; nothing on screen is a visible gradient): no image, no DOM per cell, no script, nothing animated, and it sits on the hero rather than its drifting contents, so it is still while scrolling. It is the hero's surface. If it is the first thing a viewer notices, lower the two tokens, not the structure. **It bends under the pointer** (`motion/hero-grid.tsx`): for a fine pointer that can hover, a canvas draws the same grid in the same place (cell, centre line, groove and lit edge, crossings, mask) and the CSS grid steps aside (`data-grid="live"`), because a repeating CSS background cannot bend one line. Within 2.6 cells of the pointer every point is pushed outward along the line from it, zero at the pointer, peaking about halfway and falling smoothly to zero at the edge (`(1 − r²/R²)²·r/R`, peak about a seventh of a cell), so the lines part around it like a fine mesh under a fingertip: each crossing stays on both its lines, nothing outside the field moves, and the push stays below the reach so no line can fold over its neighbour. Inside the field the grooves deepen in proportion to the pressure (`--color-groove-near`, `--color-groove-lit-near`), so the bend reads as depth; there is no glow, spotlight, cursor mark or accent. The field follows the pointer with an exponential ease (no overshoot, no spring), rises as it moves and settles back to the exact rest frame when it leaves or rests for 0.6s. The canvas draws once at rest and runs a frame loop only while the field is moving, rising or settling. Touch, reduced motion and no script keep the CSS grid.
- Colors are defined once, in `src/app/globals.css`. The tokens there, with their comments, are the reference (the `/color-test` page was removed before launch, November 2026).

### Typography: three roles, two families max

| Role | Use | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Display** | Hero, project titles, large numbers | Large, confident, tight leading, used sparingly. May be an optical size or weight range of the text family. |
| **Text** | Body, case-study prose | Highly readable editorial face, comfortable at 17–19px. |
| **Label** | Wall labels, indices, metadata, figures, code | Mono or a grotesk with excellent tabular figures. Small and precise. |

- **Two families max** (e.g. one variable family for display + text, plus one label/mono family).
- **Fluid type scale** with `clamp()`, about a 1.25 ratio on mobile and 1.333 on desktop. Steps: `label, xs, sm, base, lg, xl, 2xl, 3xl, display, display-xl`. `display-xl` is reserved for exhibit titles. One extra step, `hero`, sits outside the ratio for the reference-replication phase: the hero headline on the reference's own fluid curve (32px at 375px to 96px at 1600px), in our face and tracking.
- Body: line-height 1.5–1.6, measure **60–72ch**.
- Display: leading 0.95–1.1, negative tracking scaled to size, `text-wrap: balance`. Prose uses `text-wrap: pretty`.
- Labels: `tabular-nums`, lining figures, slight positive tracking (+0.02–0.06em). Uppercase only if the face supports it well at small sizes.
- **Optical alignment:** large display text gets a token-based negative inline offset so glyph stems, not side bearings, align to the grid. Use hanging punctuation for pull quotes.
- Real typographic characters: curly quotes, en/em dashes, `×`, `→`, non-breaking spaces before units and between index and title.

### Space & layout: the exhibition grid

- 4px base unit. Use the Tailwind spacing scale and don't invent off-scale values.
- **12-column grid** on desktop, 4 on mobile. Fluid gutters and margins (`clamp`).
- Rhythm tokens: `section-y` (between sections) and `exhibit-y` (between projects, noticeably larger, about 60–100% of the viewport height, clamped).
- Max content width about 1440px. Media may go full-bleed. Prose is constrained by measure.
- **Deliberate asymmetry:** labels and metadata sit in narrow columns (e.g. 1–3), and work and prose sit offset (e.g. 4–12 or 2–10). Never center a whole composition by default.
- **Visible grid logic, where appropriate:** hairline `rule`s at section boundaries, column-aligned label rows and index markers. The grid should be _felt_ everywhere and _seen_ only in a few places.

### Media

- Aspect-ratio tokens: `media-wide` (16:10), `media-cinema` (21:9), `media-tall` (4:5), `media-square` (1:1). Every project picks from these.
- Plate padding token: media can sit on a `surface` mat with consistent inset, like a mounted print.
- No device mockups. Show real UI cropped with intent, at real pixel density.

### Shape & elevation

- Radius: `none` by default. `sm` (2–4px) only where it mirrors the radius inside the work being shown.
- **No drop shadows.** Separate with space, hairline rules and surface tone.

### Motion tokens (roles fixed; values tuned by feel in Phase 6)

| Token | Starting value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `duration-instant` | 90ms | pressed/active feedback |
| `duration-fast` | 160ms | hover in, color, focus |
| `duration-base` | 240ms | hover out, small state changes |
| `duration-slow` | 600ms | text/section reveals |
| `duration-intro` | 1200ms | the theme switch's roll; the page entrance runs on its own timeline (§6) |
| `duration-deliberate` | 700ms | one object changing form: the text link's underline, both directions |
| `stagger` | 50ms | sequential elements |
| `ease-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` | entrances, hover in (default) |
| `ease-in-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` | movement between states |
| `ease-emphasis` | `cubic-bezier(0.83, 0, 0.17, 1)` | a long, symmetric move that rests at both ends (power4.inOut) |

GSAP uses the same values, exported from `lib/motion/gsap.ts`.

## 5. Components (visual rules)

The final pattern for presenting projects is decided in §9.3 (decision 4). The rules below hold whatever pattern is chosen.

### Interaction states (applies to everything interactive)

- Define **rest, hover, focus-visible and pressed** for every element. Add **loading/complete** where an action exists (e.g. copy email).
- **Hover and focus-visible look identical** apart from the focus ring. Keyboard users get the full experience.
- **Asymmetric timing:** states enter at `duration-fast` and leave at `duration-base`. That asymmetry is what makes them feel physical.
- **Pressed** gives tactile feedback with a tiny transform (≤ 1–2px translate or 0.98 scale) at `duration-instant`.
- **No layout shift on any state change.** Animate transform/opacity/color only. Reserve space for revealed content.
- Touch devices get pressed states and statically visible previews, never hover-only information.

### Exhibit (a project on the home page)

- One project per viewport, large media composition, generous `exhibit-y` spacing.
- **Wall label:** index (`01`), title, year, role and "medium" (the stack), set in Label style and column-aligned. Optionally add one hard number (a metric with units).
- Each exhibit uses one of a **small fixed set of compositions** (e.g. full-bleed, offset-wide, label-left/media-right). Vary them deliberately. Never repeat the same composition for consecutive projects.
- The whole exhibit is one link with a clear accessible name. Hover/focus responses are restrained: media scales ≤ 1.02 inside a fixed clip, or crossfades to a short muted loop; the index/arrow advances a few px; a `rule` strengthens. Nothing lifts or casts a shadow.

### Project index (compact list, e.g. on /about or the footer of a case study)

- Numbered rows: `01 · Title · Role · Year`, tabular, rule-separated.
- On fine pointers, hovering or focusing a row shows a preview in a **fixed, anchored slot**. It never follows the cursor.

### Case study page

- Opens with a large media plate and a precise meta block (role, timeline, team, stack, links, key numbers).
- Editorial body: prose at measure in an offset column. Sidenotes, figures and metrics sit in the margin column on desktop and go inline on mobile.
- Media breaks out of the measure to wide or full-bleed. Figures are numbered (`Fig. 03`) with Label-style captions.
- Ends with the next project presented as an exhibit preview, not a "Next →" button.

## 6. Motion language

Motion should feel **tactile, restrained and physical**: things settle into place, respond to touch, and never perform.

- **Signature moment:** a single preloader and hero entrance, one timeline (`motion/hero-intro.tsx`). The preloader is this site's own: on the brand field, a counter in Archivo Expanded 600 (§9.1) sits in a ring at the centre and runs from 01 to 100 as an odometer, its number update imitating the owner's earlier portfolio: each column is a strip of figures sliding up behind a clipped slot, the units through every number and the tens in proportion, on one curve (2.6s, `power3.inOut`), so digits leave over the top while the next rise from below; the 1 of 100 rises with the last ten as its slot opens and the number re-centres. The figures are proportional, so each digit has a slot one zero wide (the hundreds slot one 1 wide) and the count never shifts sideways. **The ring is a comet**, a line of 0.026em (about 3.5px on desktop, never less than 3px, rendered at 3px) over a faint track of itself (20%): a bright arc orbits (two turns over the count, faster as it goes) with a tail that grows from 50° to the whole circle, the tail's far end rising from clear to solid over the last twelve counts, so it catches its own tail and is the whole ring at the instant 100 lands. It is a conic mask on the ring driven by the same curve as the count (`--spin`, `--tail`, `--floor`, written by the island); at 100 the mask and the track are removed, so no seam shows. **The loader has a voice:** a status line under the counter (the label face, small capitals, a blinking block cursor after the text while it is working; the greeting has none), dry and a little self-aware, about the work that really goes into the site, from `content/site.ts` (`loader`): "Warming up the pixels", "Aligning the grid", "Kerning the numbers", "Negotiating with flexbox", "Deleting unused code", "Polishing the orange", "Almost there. Probably.", and, as 100 lands, "Hello." It rises in as the count starts (0.5s, `power3.out`), rolls up one line at each even stretch of the count, showing only as many of the loader's lines as stay up 0.5s each (five of the seven at this length, picked evenly from first to last; the copy in `site.ts` is unchanged) (found on the count's own curve, so a change lands as the number reaches it; 0.5s, `power3.out`, the same roll as the digits), and is gone just before the ring moves (0.25s). **Corner brackets** frame the screen like a viewfinder: four L-shaped marks (2.5rem, a 2px line in the field's ink) inset to the page's frame edge (16px, 24px from md), each drawing out of its own corner as the count starts (0.6s, `power3.out`, 0.05s apart, scale only); they are part of the normal layer, so the growing window cuts them away with the rest. **The AW logo** (the mark face, Deutsch Gothic, as the navbar sets it) sits centred right above the ring, as far above it as the status line is below. It is split into letters by hand (each in its own mask, the way the footer's name is) and rises in as the count starts, A then W (0.6s each, `power3.out`, 0.15s apart, from 0.15s), and stays until the growing window covers it (it is part of the normal layer, so the clip cuts it away). **When the ring starts to shrink the logo leaves the way it came:** each letter drops back down behind its mask, A then W (0.4s, `power3.in`, 0.08s apart), no fade and no scaling, the entry in reverse; it is gone before the ring lands. The reveal then starts as the ring lands, with no rest. (A version that built the name, ALWIN, and folded it down to AW, a lockup that swapped the AW for a small "Alwin" or full name, and a fall that shrank and faded the mark, were built and set aside.) It claims nothing it is not doing: no fake progress or status. **Inside the ring the colours are inverted:** a second copy of the counter (a dark disc, orange figures, rolled in step with the first) is clipped to a disc 0.06em inside the ring's line, so the comet is an outline around the disc with a thin orange gap between them. As the ring contracts the disc shrinks with it, its edge sweeping across the 1 and the last 0 and handing them back to their normal colours; over the last 0.3s of the contraction the disc fades out, so everything is back to normal as the ring lands on the zero. 100 holds for 0.3s, then **the ring contracts onto the middle zero** (0.75s, `power3.inOut`), its corners tightening from a circle to the zero's rounded rectangle; the digits never move. The zero's centre is read from the rendered cell when the contraction starts, and its size and corner radius are the typeface's measurements (hero-intro.css, read by the island as custom properties: a rounded rectangle 0.6525em by 0.7125em with 0.30em corners, which fits to within 1% where an ellipse does not). The ring lies on its outer contour and inside its ink, so the two read as one shape: verified by pixel diff at 360, 768, 1024 and 1440, where the ring adds nothing the zero does not already draw. (A zoom finish, scaling "100" up about the middle zero until it filled the ring, was built and set aside.) **The reveal is one growth on one curve** (1.4s, `power4.inOut`), starting 0.1s before the ring lands so the ring never stops: a window onto the hero opens from a point at the zero's centre, grows out through the zero, taking its ink with it, to the ring's inner edge, and from there the ring and the window carry on together, each size inflated by the same amount (the rounded rectangle slides into a circle), past the farthest corner. It never stops or changes gear at the zero. The window is an `evenodd` clip-path on the field, rewritten each frame; when it has fully revealed the hero (about 5s) the hero entrance in §9.3 (decision 3) plays, unchanged, timed from that moment. Deliberate exceptions to transform/opacity: the ring's size, corner radius and mask, and the hundreds slot's width, are animated (one absolutely positioned element and one inline slot; the ring's line must stay constant, which scaling would not), and the field's clip-path is rewritten each frame of the reveal, as the earlier sweep's was. It plays on every full load of the home page, is skipped for reduced motion and for deep links (the inline script in `layout.tsx`), runs about 7s to the last settle (the hero is fully revealed at about 5s, the length the owner chose after trying 3.4s), over the 1.2s budget by explicit request, and **plays once per browser tab**: the inline head script skips it once `sessionStorage` holds `intro-seen` (set by the island when the intro starts; if storage is blocked it simply plays again), so a reload or a return to the home page plays the hero's entrance alone (`html.is-enter`, set by the same head script before first paint). **It is CSS, not script** (`hero.css`): the same beats and curves as the intro's entrance (the accent and ink blocks in one continuous pass, the navbar sliding down, the action and the scroll cue lifting in), started on the first frame the browser paints, as transforms only, so the compositor runs them smoothly while the page is still starting up; `motion/hero-intro.tsx` only drops the class when it is done (1.3s) or on any input. The cells are set up as soon as their module runs, on the server-rendered canvas, before React hydrates, and on a reload are drawn once, whole, under the blocks, so the accent block's exit reveals them. **Why:** on a reload the entrance ran while the page was starting up. Driven by GSAP after hydration it began only after about 0.9s of empty grid in development (measured in the owner's browser); then, as CSS that switched `transform-origin`, it was held on the busy main thread and stalled a third of the way across; and the cells, printed a frame at a time, dropped to a few frames a second (owner: smooth after the preloader, 2fps on a reload). Now the wipe starts at about 0.17–0.25s and the cells are ready before the reveal even at a 4x slower CPU in production (0.47s against 0.81s). With script the plain name stays transparent until the cells take over (`hero.css`, on the line, with a 1.5s fallback that shows it if they never do). It was 3.9s of count and 6.6s to the hero on every load, so a visitor returning to the home page paid the full sequence each time; shortened and limited at the owner's request, every beat kept, and any wheel, touch or key input fast-forwards it. Dark mode: the ring is black on the orange field and the dark hero behind it is nearly the same colour, so while it opens it reads as a dark circle growing from the zero. Replaces the good-fella.com squares-and-sweep preloader.
- **Media:** project images are not revealed and do not move; on a fine pointer a selection hatch fades over the cover (Selected Work, §5 Chrome).
- **Text reveals:** subtle 8–16px translate plus opacity on headings and labels, with short stagger. Never on body paragraphs. **Exception, by reference (Skills, §5 Chrome):** the home Skills list uses the reference's own reveal, lines rising out of masks over their full height, and their paragraphs fade up 24px once.
- **Micro-interactions:** link underlines, exhibit hover, index-row previews, copy confirmation, focus transitions. This is where most of the craft budget goes.
- **One thing moves at a time.** Don't run competing animations in the same viewport.
- **Never:** bounce/elastic easing, parallax on text, parallax as a default, scroll-jacking, looping decorative animation, cursor-driven effects, anything that delays reading content.
- **Reduced motion:** everything appears in its final state instantly. Color/opacity fades of ≤ 150ms are acceptable. Video loops show their poster frame.

## 7. Responsive

- Mobile-first. Design at 360px, 768px, 1024px, 1440px and 1920px+.
- Layouts reflow; they don't just shrink. Asymmetric desktop compositions become a single strong column, and media stays large.
- Touch targets ≥ 44px. No hover-only information: anything shown on hover is also reachable by focus or tap.
- Use `dvh`/`svh` for full-height sections and never rely on `100vh` on mobile.
- At 1920px+, add space and scale media. Don't stretch line lengths.

## 8. Accessibility (visual)

- Focus ring: 2px `focus` outline with offset, visible on the page in both modes and on top of media.
- Never convey meaning with color alone.
- Respect user font-size settings: type uses `rem`, not px.
- Text must stay readable at 200% zoom and 320px width without horizontal scroll.
- Video loops are muted, captioned when they carry meaning, and pausable.

The name is hinted onto the grid, the way a pixel face is drawn. Each letter becomes its own bitmap of cells: measured from its outline and scaled a hair (a few percent at most) so its width and the capital height are whole numbers of cells, with the baseline on a cell row; a cell is drawn, whole, where the letter covers at least half of it, so a left and a right edge round the same way (no partial squares). A letter whose outline is symmetric is made exactly symmetric in cells (A and W mirrored, N turned half way). A counter (the A's closed hole) is redrawn as a tiered pixel tree, centred and ending on its own bottom width so the crossbar and legs do not move: 1, 3, 5, then 3, 5 cells wide at 1440 (traced, it was 1, 1, 3, 3, 5, a narrow shaft over a wider base that read as something else; owner's request). The letters are then spaced by their contours, row by row, as a type designer kerns: each sits as close as it can with 2 empty cells to its neighbour in every row both have ink, so the W tucks in over the L's arm where box-to-box spacing left a hole between them, and straight pairs (I and N) keep exactly 2 cells. Without the hinting, stems straddled the columns wherever the font fell: twin stems came out 6 and 7 cells wide, the A's counter was lopsided, the gaps were 2 or 3 cells and L–W had a hole (owner's notes: "unevenness and bad distribution", "the center of A", "the space between L and W"). The drawn letters, not the text box, are centred on the hero's centre line (the face's advance leaves room after the last letter): they move by whole cells to the step nearest the centre, so they stay on the grid (measured: exact at 1440 and on a phone, 9px off at 1920, where the letters' width is not a whole number of cells). The heading stays real text (its glyphs go transparent once the cells have drawn). **Entrance:** the intro's own: as the wipe's accent and ink blocks leave the line left to right, the cells print in behind them in the same direction (each grows from nothing over 320ms, ease-out cubic; the sweep crosses the name in 650ms), started the moment the intro turns the line on. **Hover** (fine pointer): within 2.2 grid squares of the pointer the cells shrink on a smooth bell ((1 − r²/R²)², up to 82% at the centre), opening the letters into a halftone, ink only (the site's pointer mark is the accent); the field follows with the grid's own easings (0.2 follow, 0.16 rise, 0.08 settle, no overshoot), and while it is over the name the grid's bend stands down (`data-cells-hover`). The frame loop runs only during the entrance and while the field moves. **Drawing on the grid (touch screens; owner's choice from four studied touch interactions; `motion/hero-draw.tsx`):** phones had none of the hero's interaction (the grid's bend and the name's halftone both answer hover), and a phone's name, kept on one line, leaves most of the first screen as empty grid. On a touch screen (no hover, a coarse pointer) a finger dragged across the hero lights the cells it passes in the ink colour: a third of a grid square each (the desktop name's cell), on the grid's own lines, a 2 × 2-cell brush filled in between touch points so a swipe draws a continuous line, and each cell fades out over 0.6s (it holds, then eases away). Every cell grows in from the centre of its square over 0.07s (an ease-out, as the name's cells print), so nothing pops. Drag only: nothing is drawn until the finger has travelled half a cell, and the trail then starts where it touched down, so a tap leaves no mark (a tap pulse was built in several versions and removed at the owner's request). The trail was 1.5s and cleared too slowly; it is 0.6s. The trail is a canvas over the grid and under the copy, so it never covers the name or the button or takes a tap, and it stays put while the copy drifts on scroll. The listeners are passive, so the page always scrolls as it would (a vertical swipe scrolls and leaves a trail; there is no drawing mode to get stuck in). A prompt, "Drag to draw" (label face, muted, `hero.drawHint`), led by three small ink cells lighting up in turn (the frame button's chevron chase: 1.2s, 120ms apart), sits in the empty space between the action and the scroll cue, appears 1.2s after the page is ready, and goes for the visit after the first stroke. The canvas draws only while a trail is fading. Nothing for a mouse, for reduced motion, without script, or while the intro plays; decorative and `aria-hidden`. **Departure, flagged:** §6 rules out cursor-driven effects; this is touch-driven and deliberate, by the owner's choice (the alternatives, stacking the name as AL / WIN or letting it bleed past the edges, were set aside).
